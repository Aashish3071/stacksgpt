## What You're Building

An audience growth prediction and content trend forecasting engine. Most marketing teams look backwards, reviewing follower counts and engagement rates days after campaigns have concluded.

This workflow takes historical follower snapshots, post impressions, and comment velocity from LinkedIn, Twitter/X, and YouTube. A Python or JavaScript Code node calculates compounding growth rates and 30-day forward projections. A language model reasoning step identifies which hook formats, formats, and topics correlate with breakout subscriber growth, generating a forward-looking editorial forecast for your content creators.

## What Predictive Modeling and Language Models Can Handle

Manual spreadsheets require hours of formula updating and lack qualitative trend synthesis. Automating this system provides:

1. **Automated Follower Snapshots**: Collects daily follower totals and net additions across brand and founder profiles.
2. **Growth Trajectory Modeling**: Calculates moving averages, momentum velocity, and projected end-of-quarter subscriber counts.
3. **Format Correlation Analysis**: Isolates which specific asset structures (carousels, long-form teardowns, video shorts) yield the highest follower conversion.
4. **Editorial Focus Directives**: Recommends upcoming editorial themes with high predicted reach based on recent audience acceleration.

## What You Need

1. Social platform APIs or scraping webhooks (LinkedIn API, Twitter API v2, or RapidAPI data endpoints).
2. A Google Sheets workbook to record historical metrics and forward forecasts.
3. An OpenAI API key with access to GPT-4o.
4. An active n8n instance.

## How to Set It Up

1. Create a Google Sheet with tabs for Historical Metrics and Growth Forecasts.
2. In n8n, set up authentication for your social data sources or RapidAPI connectors.
3. Connect your Google Sheets service account and OpenAI credentials.
4. Import the provided workflow JSON template into n8n.
5. In the Code node, verify the projection formulas for your current target milestones.
6. Trigger a manual execution to populate baseline metrics and generate your first forecast briefing.

## The Core Workflows

1. **Data Harvesting**: Fetches total followers, post impressions, profile views, and engagement percentages across tracked channels.
2. **Velocity Calculations**: Subtracts previous period baselines to calculate 7-day rolling growth rates and retention efficiency.
3. **Trend Extrapolation**: Runs linear and compounding regression projections to estimate 30-day and 90-day audience size.
4. **Strategic Synthesis**: GPT-4o analyzes the quantitative growth figures alongside recent top-performing topics to explain what drove the inflection points.
5. **Briefing Output**: Appends the projections to Google Sheets and dispatches an executive summary to marketing leadership.

## What Stays Under Your Control

- **Projection Models**: Switch between conservative linear models or aggressive compounding curves in the Code node.
- **Milestone Goals**: Define target quarterly thresholds to track whether your channels are ahead of or behind schedule.
- **Channel Weighting**: Prioritize high-converting business channels like LinkedIn over top-of-funnel viral channels.

## Customize It

1. **Email Newsletter Integration**: Sync Beehiiv or Substack subscriber metrics to compare social impressions against email list conversion.
2. **Sentiment Weighting**: Incorporate positive versus neutral comment sentiment to adjust predicted retention scores.
3. **Multi-Creator Benchmarking**: Compare your executive team growth rates against key competitors in the same vertical.

## Keep It Reliable

1. **Outlier Dampening**: Exclude viral anomalies (e.g. single posts with 100x average views) from baseline calculations to prevent unrealistic forecasts.
2. **API Rate Quotas**: Stagger social API scraping requests across 3-minute intervals to stay safely below platform rate limits.
3. **Data Freshness Checks**: Ensure the timestamp of retrieved social metrics is within 24 hours before computing moving averages.
