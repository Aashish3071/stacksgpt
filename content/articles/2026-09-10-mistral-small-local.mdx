Running an AI model on your own computer is not new. What has held it back is
that, on the hardware most people actually own, the experience was slow enough
that they tried it once and went back to a hosted tool. Mistral's announcement
is a claim about that gap closing.

The distinction matters most for people who currently cannot use these tools at
work at all. Anyone handling client files, medical notes, case records or
unreleased financial information has faced the same conflict: the tool is
useful, but pasting the document into someone else's website breaks a
confidentiality rule, and sometimes a law. A model that runs on the machine in
front of you removes that conflict, because there is no upload step to justify
to anyone.

What Mistral has not addressed is equally worth noting. The company published no
benchmark comparison against its own hosted range or against competitors, so
claims about capability cannot be checked. It announced no price, and did not
say whether the model will stay free or eventually sit behind a paid tier. It
also gave no indication of how frequently it will be updated, which matters if
you intend to build a working habit around it.

For readers weighing it up, the honest position is that the privacy benefit is
concrete and verifiable, because you can simply disconnect from the network and
watch it keep working. The capability question is not, at least until someone
publishes independent testing.
