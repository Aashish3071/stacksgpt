Local models have been possible for a couple of years, but they were slow enough
on ordinary hardware that most people tried one, waited, and went back to a
hosted tool. The practical change Mistral is claiming here is speed on the kind
of laptop people actually own — fast enough that you stop noticing the work is
happening on your own machine.

That matters most for people who currently cannot use AI at work at all. Anyone
handling client files, medical notes, case records or unreleased financials has
had to choose between the tool and the confidentiality rule. A model that runs
locally removes the choice, because there is no upload step to worry about.

The trade-off is capability. Mistral has not published benchmarks against the
larger frontier models, and in practice small local models struggle with nuanced
creative writing, long multi-turn context, and complex reasoning. But for
straightforward local tasks — summarizing meeting records, formatting notes, and
extracting action items — it gives professionals a genuinely private alternative.
